Introduction to Unit PS5

What You Will Learn

Can you name the different public services that support people where you live? Every day people use public services — to get help in an emergency, to receive crime prevention advice, or to seek medical attention. There are also many other services, including education services and health and safety inspections.

In this unit, you will find out about the different public services in your area and what purpose they serve. You will investigate common features such as uniforms and practices such as drill. You will then produce a document showing what you have found out.

Learning Aim A Find out information about different public services

You will research different types of public services, how they work, and what the public expects from them.

Learning Aim B Produce information about public services in your area, showing how they meet the needs of the public

You will create a document that presents what you have found about local public services and their users.

Glossary of Key Terms

Key Words for Unit PS5

Learn these words before you start your work — they will help you understand the activities and assessment tasks

Term Definition
Armed Services The military organisations that defend a country. In the UK these are the Army, Royal Navy and Royal Air Force.
Central Government The national government based in Westminster that runs services for the whole of the UK, such as the armed forces and national defence.
Ceremonial Dress A special, formal version of a uniform worn for official occasions such as parades, state funerals or remembrance events.
Charity A non-profit organisation that works to help people or a cause without making money for its owners. Also called a voluntary organisation.
Drill A military practice where personnel march and move in formation together. Used in public services to build discipline, teamwork, and the habit of following orders.
Emergency Services Services that respond immediately when lives are at risk. In the UK these are the Police, Fire Service and Ambulance Service.
Entitlement Something a person has a legal right to receive. Many public services are an entitlement — the public has a right to use them.
Environmental Services Public services that protect and maintain the environment, such as refuse collection, recycling, and environmental health inspections.
Functions The main jobs or purposes of a public service, for example defence, protection, justice, transportation, health and wellbeing, or maintaining security.
HAZCHEM Short for Hazardous Chemicals. A specialist area of the fire service that deals with dangerous chemical or toxic substance incidents.
Health Service A public service that looks after people's medical needs, including hospitals, GP surgeries, ambulances, and mental health services.
Individual Citizen A single person using a public service for their own specific need, for example a victim of crime reporting to the police.
Justice The fair enforcement of the law. A function of services such as the police, courts and probation service.
Law and Order A type of public service concerned with upholding the law and dealing with criminal behaviour, for example the police and courts.
Leisure Services Public services that provide sport, recreation and cultural activities, such as libraries, leisure centres and parks.
Local Government A council that runs services for a specific local area, such as a county or city. Examples: refuse collection, local parks, social services.
Maintaining Security A function of public services that involves monitoring threats and keeping people and the country safe from harm.
Non-Uniformed Service A public service whose workers do not wear a standard uniform at work. Examples include social workers, council officers and library staff.
Primary Source Information you collect yourself directly, for example by interviewing a firefighter or observing a public service in action.
Protection A function of public services that involves keeping people safe from crime, fire, accident and harm.
Provider The organisation responsible for funding and running a public service. Providers include central government, local government, regional government and the third sector.
Public Service An organisation that is set up to serve everyone in society. Public services help keep people safe, healthy, and educated.
Regional Government A government body that covers a specific region, such as the Welsh Government or the Scottish Government.
Relevant Information Information that is directly connected to what you are researching and helps answer your question.
Research Log A written record of your research: what sources you used, how you searched for information, and what you found.
Secondary Source Information that has already been collected and published by someone else, such as a website, book, leaflet or report.
Section of the Community A specific group of people within a larger population who share a common characteristic, for example elderly people or young people.
Social Services A public service that supports vulnerable people in the community, including children at risk, older people, and adults with disabilities.
Third Sector Organisations that are not run by the government and do not make a profit. Includes charities, voluntary organisations and community groups.
Transportation A function of public services concerned with maintaining roads, rail, bridges and other infrastructure so people and goods can move safely.
Uniformed Service A public service whose workers wear a standard uniform at work. Examples include the police, fire service, ambulance service and armed forces.
Valid Source A source of information that is reliable, accurate and up to date. For example, an official government or NHS website is more valid than an anonymous blog.
Value for Money The idea that a public service should be worth the cost to taxpayers — delivering good quality outcomes without wasting public money.
Whole Community All the people in an area using a public service together, for example an entire town benefiting from refuse collection or flood response.

Knowledge Hub 1 — Types of Public Services

What are Public Services?

Public services are organisations that are provided to support everyone in the UK. They help keep people safe, healthy, and educated. Some are run by the government; others are run by charities or voluntary groups.

Types of Public Service

TypeWhat it doesExamples
Armed ServicesDefend the country and respond to crises abroadArmy, Royal Navy, Royal Air Force
Emergency ServicesRespond quickly when lives are at riskPolice, Fire Service, Ambulance
Law and OrderUphold the law and deal with crimePolice, Courts, Probation Service
HealthLook after people when they are ill or injuredNHS hospitals, GP surgeries, NHS 111
Social ServicesSupport vulnerable people in the communityChildren's services, adult care services
Leisure ServicesProvide sport, recreation, and cultural activitiesLibraries, leisure centres, parks
EnvironmentalProtect and maintain the environmentRefuse collection, recycling, environmental health

Who Provides Public Services?

ProviderWhat they do
Central GovernmentRuns national services (e.g. NHS, army, national police)
Local GovernmentRuns local services (e.g. bin collection, local parks, libraries)
Regional GovernmentRuns devolved services (e.g. Welsh Government, Scottish Government)
Third SectorCharities and voluntary organisations (e.g. St John Ambulance, RNLI)

Functions of Public Services

  • Defence — protecting the country from threats
  • Protection — keeping people safe from crime, fire and harm
  • Justice — enforcing the law and dealing with offenders
  • Transportation — maintaining roads, rail and infrastructure
  • Health and wellbeing — providing medical care and support
  • Maintaining security — monitoring threats and keeping order

Knowledge Hub 2 — Ways of Working

Uniformed and Non-Uniformed Services

Public services can be divided into uniformed and non-uniformed services.

Uniformed ServicesNon-Uniformed Services
Wear a standard uniform at work. Examples: Police, Fire Service, Ambulance, Armed Forces Do not wear a standard uniform. Examples: Social workers, council officers, probation workers

Why Do Some Services Wear Uniforms?

  • Identification — the public can easily recognise who they are
  • Authority — a uniform shows the person has official powers
  • Safety — specialist uniform protects workers (e.g. fire kit, HAZCHEM suits)
  • Team identity — uniform promotes a sense of belonging and discipline
  • Ceremonial dress — used for formal occasions to show respect and tradition

Common Practices in Public Services

  • Drill — practising marching and movements together. Drill teaches teamwork, discipline, and the importance of following orders.
  • Team activities — exercises and training that build trust between colleagues and improve communication.
  • Briefings — short meetings before a shift or operation to share important information.

Knowledge Hub 3 — Requirements and Expectations of the Public

Who Uses Public Services?

The people who use public services are called users or members of the public. They include:

  • Individual citizens — e.g. a family needing housing support
  • Sections of the community — e.g. older people needing care services
  • Whole communities — e.g. a town needing refuse collection

What Do the Public Expect from Public Services?

ExpectationWhat it means
Value for moneyServices should be worth the taxes people pay
ConvenienceServices should be accessible and easy to use
QualityServices should be carried out to a high standard
Timely responseServices should respond quickly, especially in emergencies
Recognise diversityServices should treat everyone fairly regardless of background

Most public services are an entitlement — this means people have a right to receive them. However, limited resources (money, staff, equipment) can affect what is available.

Knowledge Hub 5 — Presenting Information

How to Present Information About Public Services

For your assessment you will need to produce a document that presents what you have found out about public services. This could be a leaflet, poster, brochure, or word-processed document.

What to Include

  • The name of each public service
  • Whether it is uniformed or non-uniformed
  • Who the main provider is (central government, local government, etc.)
  • The functions of the service (what it does)
  • Details about uniforms and common practices
  • Who the service works with — individual citizens, sections of the community, whole communities
  • How the service meets the requirements and expectations of the public

Tips for a Good Document

  • Use clear headings for each public service
  • Keep language simple and easy to read
  • Use bullet points or short paragraphs
  • Include information about at least THREE public services
  • Include at least one uniformed and one non-uniformed service
  • Show how services link to the needs of the public

Audience Awareness

Think about who will read your document. For a community awareness exhibition, your audience will include members of the public who may not know much about public services. Keep your language clear and avoid jargon.

What Does This Service Do? (Functions)

  • Protection — keeping people safe from crime and harm
  • Justice — investigating crime and enforcing the law
  • Maintaining security — monitoring threats and keeping public order

Uniform Information

  • Officers wear a standard navy-blue uniform including a high-visibility jacket, utility belt, and custodian helmet or cap
  • Uniform helps the public identify officers quickly and shows their authority
  • Specialist units (e.g. armed response) wear additional protective equipment including body armour

Who Uses This Service?

  • Individual citizens — e.g. a person who has been a victim of crime
  • Sections of the community — e.g. young people at risk of becoming involved in crime
  • Whole communities — e.g. a town experiencing a rise in antisocial behaviour

How Does It Meet the Needs of the Public?

  • Timely response: 999 calls for serious incidents receive a rapid response, usually within minutes
  • Quality: Officers are trained to professional standards set by the College of Policing
  • Value for money: The service is funded by council tax and central government grants
  • Recognising diversity: The force has equality policies and officers receive diversity training
Did You Know? Dyfed-Powys Police covers the largest geographic area of any police force in England and Wales — over 4,500 square miles.
